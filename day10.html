Before dawn you drift silently to the collpa - a riverbank clay wall where, as the light rises, dozens to hundreds of parrots and macaws descend in a screeching kaleidoscope to eat mineral clay. It is one of South America's great wildlife spectacles.

Then into the reserve proper: a muddy trail and a wooden canoe deliver you onto Lake Sandoval, a palm-ringed oxbow where a resident family of giant otters hunts in formation and hoatzins - prehistoric punk-crested birds - crash about in the branches. After dinner, a torch-lit walk reveals the small night shift: tarantulas, tree frogs, leaf-cutter highways.

Field notes

  • Clay-lick activity is best in dry season and mornings only - May is good
  • Sandoval sits inside the National Reserve: passport needed at the SERNANP post
  • Rubber boots (lodge-issued) obligatory - the trail is real mud
  • One binocular pair per 2-3 people transforms the day

Photo tips

  • Collpa: long zoom, shoot bursts when the flock lifts
  • Sandoval mirror-water at noon: symmetrical palm reflections
  • Otters: patience + silence beat any lens

Gear for today

  • Zoom camera / best phone zoom in the group
  • Dry bag again - canoes splash
