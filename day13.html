The lake makes its own weather and its own worlds. First the Uros: dozens of springy platforms woven wholesale from totora reed - islands, houses, boats and all - endlessly re-layered by the families who anchor them.

Then two and a half hours of open water to Taquile, where society still runs on the Inca code (don't steal, don't lie, don't be lazy) and the men do the knitting - their hats encode marital status. Lunch is grilled trout on a terrace above impossibly blue water, Bolivia's snow cordillera floating on the horizon.

Field notes

  • The lake sun is the trip's most dangerous: SPF50, glasses, hat - reapply
  • Small soles for Uros crafts and the reed-boat ride (~2 EUR)
  • Taquile's stone steps at 3,950 m - slowest pace of the trip
  • Mornings on the water are freezing; layers that shed by noon

Photo tips
