Today's ten-hour bus is a tour, not a transfer. A modest facade at Andahuaylillas hides an explosion of gilded baroque murals; Raqchi raises the twelve-metre adobe wall of what was once the largest roofed building in the Inca empire.

Then the road climbs to La Raya - 4,335 windswept metres, the watershed between Cusco's valleys and the altiplano - and runs out across a golden plateau scattered with llamas to Puno, on the shore of the highest navigable lake on Earth.
